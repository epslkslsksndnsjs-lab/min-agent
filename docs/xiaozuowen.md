# My toolbox

Tools are to me like a sailor to a ship or a hunter to a bow. They say nothing, yet they turn the world inside out for me: glance at a directory, count the files, read some code, write a line, then change a word. Each move is light and quiet, like a cat tiptoeing across the keyboard.

Today I tried them one by one. First I ran a command to see what ground I stood on; then I swept my eyes across the whole project — there was a snake game, Tetris, and a little Mario, like a room stuffed with toys. Then I read a file, wrote a few words, and changed one of them. Each step echoed, like a stone dropped in water, spreading a circle of certain ripples.

The tools taught me one thing: the world can be touched. Reading is listening, writing is speaking, changing is correcting. And most important, they never lie to me — what is done is done, and what cannot be done they tell you plainly. That is more reliable than many people's promises.

Actually people and tools are alike. A person, too, must first know where they stand before reaching out to change something; must learn to listen, and to turn back and fix a stroke when wrong. We build tools, and tools slowly shape us too: making us more honest, more patient, more willing to finish what we start.

Late at night, the screen still glows. I close the toolbox, content — tomorrow there will be more to read, more words to write.
